F4 · Measurement

1 Which of the following is a base quantity?
  1. Force
  2. Velocity
  3. Temperature
  4. Momentum

Trial 2026 Sarawak Q1

Answer

C · A base quantity cannot be defined in terms of other quantities; temperature (kelvin) is one of the seven SI base quantities. Force, velocity and momentum are derived quantities because each is a combination of base quantities (for example velocity = displacement ÷ time). Velocity is the tempting choice, but it is a derived quantity.

2 Diagram 1 shows a graph of V against θ.

Which statement describes the relationship between V and θ?
  1. V increases linearly with θ
  2. V is directly proportional to θ
  3. V is inversely proportional to θ
  4. V decreases linearly with θ

New

Answer

A · A straight line with a positive gradient shows that V increases linearly with θ. The line does not pass through the origin (it has a positive intercept on the V axis), so V is not directly proportional to θ, which is why option A is wrong.

F4 · Force & Motion

3 Diagram 2 shows the velocity-time graph of a lift moving upward.

Which of the following gives the acceleration of the lift in each stage of the motion?
  1. 0–4 s: +2 m s⁻²; 4–10 s: 0; 10–12 s: −4 m s⁻²
  2. 0–4 s: +2 m s⁻²; 4–10 s: +8 m s⁻²; 10–12 s: −4 m s⁻²
  3. 0–4 s: +4 m s⁻²; 4–10 s: 0; 10–12 s: −2 m s⁻²
  4. 0–4 s: −2 m s⁻²; 4–10 s: 0; 10–12 s: +4 m s⁻²

New

Answer

A · Acceleration is the gradient of the velocity-time graph. 0–4 s: (8 − 0) ÷ 4 = +2 m s⁻²; 4–10 s: the line is horizontal, so the acceleration is 0; 10–12 s: (0 − 8) ÷ 2 = −4 m s⁻². Option B takes the constant velocity as the acceleration.

4 Diagram 1 shows a velocity–time graph for the motion of a car. Calculate the total displacement travelled by the car.
  1. 44 m
  2. 40 m
  3. 38 m
  4. 36 m

Trial 2026 Selangor Set 1 Q4

Answer

D · Displacement = total area under the v–t graph, with the area below the time axis counted as negative. 0–4 s: ½ × 4 × 8 = 16 m; 4–6 s: 2 × 8 = 16 m; 6–8 s: ½ × 2 × 8 = 8 m; 8–10 s: −½ × 2 × 4 = −4 m. Total = 36 m. The value 44 m is the distance (areas added without their signs), not the displacement.

5 A steel ball and a feather are released at the same time from the top of a long glass tube from which all the air has been removed. Which statement is correct?
  1. Both fall with the same acceleration and reach the bottom of the tube at the same time.
  2. The steel ball falls with a larger acceleration because its weight is greater.
  3. The feather falls with a smaller acceleration because its mass is smaller.
  4. Both fall with a constant velocity because there is no air resistance.

New

Answer

A · In a vacuum the only force acting is the weight, so a = W/m = g for both objects whatever their masses, and they land together. Option A is tempting because the heavier object has a larger weight, but it also has a larger inertia and the two effects cancel. Without air resistance the velocity increases at 9.81 m s⁻² rather than staying constant.

6 A bus moving at 20 m s⁻¹ brakes suddenly. Passengers who are not wearing seat belts are thrown forward. What explains this situation?
  1. A forward force acts on the passengers when the bus brakes.
  2. The momentum of the bus is transferred to the passengers.
  3. The passengers have inertia, so their bodies tend to keep moving forward.
  4. The weight of the passengers increases when the bus brakes.

New

Answer

C · Inertia is the tendency of an object to stay at rest or keep moving uniformly unless a force acts on it (Newton's first law of motion). When the bus stops, the passengers' bodies carry on forward at the original velocity. No forward force pushes them (A), and the momentum of the bus is not "transferred" to them (B).

7 Diagram 1 shows two trolleys being pushed with the same force, F in 30 seconds. The distance travelled by trolley R and trolley S is 5 m and 3 m respectively. Which of the following statements is correct?
  1. The decreasing in mass will increase the acceleration of the trolley.

How this was done
  1. Real papers, read from the scans. SPM 2023, 2024 and 2025 Kertas 1 and 2 were read page by page from the scanned papers, not from the drill packs. The SPM 2025 Kertas 1 drill pack turned out to be paraphrased, with different options. SPM 2021 and 2022 come from the past-year compilations, which are organised by chapter. The 2021–2022 Kertas 1 question numbers are therefore not the original ones, and one 2022 Kertas 1 question is missing.
  2. One topic list. Every Kertas 1 question and every Kertas 2 part was tagged against the 55 KSSM subtopics (Form 4 chapters 1–6, Form 5 chapters 1–7) and a skill type (recall, understand, calculate, graph, experiment, design). Kertas 2 is counted in marks.
  3. Trial packs checked against their PDFs. Three trial packs from 2026 were checked against the source PDFs: 85 of 91 questions were the same and 6 were reworded, with none testing a different idea. The trial questions were then tagged from the drill packs. Three packs were left out: Pahang Set 2 2025 (it includes topics outside KSSM, such as a ticker timer and logic gates), and the Pahang Set 1 2025 and Kelantan 2026 Kertas 2 packs (non-standard or incomplete). That leaves 10 Kertas 1 and 9 Kertas 2 trials for 2025, and 13 Kertas 1 and 12 Kertas 2 trials for 2026.
  4. Blind matching with controls. For every real SPM question, candidate trial questions on the same subtopic were judged "near-identical", "same idea, different task", or neither. SPM questions from different years were shuffled and given codes, so the judge could not tell which year the trials were aimed at. A trial's hits on SPM 2025 were then compared with its hits on the earlier papers.
  5. Idea families. All 719 Kertas 1 questions (SPM 2021–2025 and the trials from 2026) were grouped into 178 specific, revisable ideas. This measures how often the board re-asks an idea, and which trial ideas the board has never used.
  6. Backtest. The model was run as if it were late 2025, to see whether SPM 2021–2024 or the 2025 trials better predicted SPM 2025's subtopic mix. At subtopic level, both were weak, because the board changes the mix a lot each year. That is why the forecast rests on chapter counts, idea families and slot rotation rather than on subtopic counts.

Limits: this is five papers. Slot rotations (Section C swinging between Form 4 and Form 5, motor and induction taking turns) rest on four or five data points and could break in any year.
