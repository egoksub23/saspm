Kertas 1 — what to expect
  • Fixed
    About 1–2 questions per chapter, every chapter. 40 questions across 28 chapters, so most chapters get one or two. Form 4 has taken a growing share: 21, 20, 23 and 25 of 40.
  • Re-ask
    Old ideas come back more each year: 2, 6 and then 15 questions repeated an idea from an earlier SPM paper. The Kertas 1 tab lists the 20 ideas set in two or more years. Osmosis in a Visking tube came in all four; guttation in three in a row.
  • Due
    Set in 2023 or 2024, missing in 2025, and in 4+ trials of 2026: test cross 1:1, gene mutation (sickle cell, thalassaemia), CO₂ and chemoreceptors during exercise, the lymphatic system.
  • Less
    Plant Nutrition is a Kertas 2 topic. It had no Kertas 1 question in 2022–2025, while the trials give it 1.6 a paper. Biodiversity had none in 2025 after 1, 3 and 2, so expect it back.
  • Style
    SPM asks more graphs and fewer plain recalls than the trials. SPM papers have 3–8 graph or data questions and 11–13 recall questions; the trials average 2 and 17. Practise reading graphs and diagrams; about 20 SPM questions a year hang on a diagram.

Which of the following represents J and K?
  1. J: Petri dish  |  K: Place into a special bin
  2. J: Hardened agar  |  K: Sterilise in an autoclave and dispose immediately
  3. J: Blood  |  K: Decontaminate by autoclave before disposal
  4. J: Animal organ  |  K: Pack in biohazard plastic bag, sterilise in an autoclave and place into biohazard bin

Trial 2026 Melaka Q1

Answer

A · Category A is sharp waste (a syringe), so it goes into a special puncture-resistant bin, which gives K. Category B is solid, non-sharp waste that has touched microorganisms, such as a Petri dish, which is packed in an autoclave-resistant biohazard bag and sterilised, which gives J. Option D is tempting, but animal organs belong to Category C and blood is a liquid (Category D); each has its own method.

F4 · 2 Cell Biology

2 Diagram 1 shows a cell component. Which of the following is the adaptation of the component to the function of the cell?
  1. Store water and dissolved substances
  2. Contains green pigments to absorb light
  3. Controls movement of substances in and out of the cell
  4. Has a large surface area to generate more energy

Trial 2026 Kedah Q4

Answer

D · Diagram 1 shows a mitochondrion: a double-membrane organelle whose inner membrane is folded into cristae. The folded cristae give a large surface area for the enzymes of cellular respiration, so more ATP can be generated. Storing water and dissolved substances is the job of the vacuole, green pigment is found in the chloroplast, and controlling movement of substances in and out of the cell is the job of the plasma membrane.

3 The following information is about the final stage in an enzyme production.

"R moves towards the plasma membrane and fuses with it to release S."

What are R and S?
  1. R – Transport vesicle; S – Intracellular enzymes
  2. R – Secretory vesicle; S – Intracellular enzymes
  3. R – Transport vesicle; S – Extracellular enzymes
  4. R – Secretory vesicle; S – Extracellular enzymes

Trial 2026 Perak Q7

Answer

D · Extracellular enzymes are made on the rough ER, then modified and packaged by the Golgi apparatus into secretory vesicles. The secretory vesicle moves to the plasma membrane and fuses with it (exocytosis), releasing the extracellular enzymes outside the cell.
